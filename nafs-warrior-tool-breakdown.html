Introduction · Nafs Warrior

What is Nafs Warrior?

Nafs Warrior (nafswarriorofficial.vercel.app) is a mentorship site for the Muslim who is stuck in a repeated sin — music, porn, masturbation, or any guilty habit — and wants a practical, shame-free way out. Its slogan is the mission: “Win the war within.”

What it is
A site of small, built tools

Not a wall of lectures. Each tool is an interactive page built for one exact moment of the journey — read a paragraph a day, answer in your notebook, tick steps off, follow a protocol. Read when you need it; tap it when it matters.

How it works
One tool per moment

The Music Tool walks 21 days in three weeks. The Urge Button and Panic Controller rescue the exact moment temptation or panic hits. The Excuses pages dismantle the inner voice. Sabr, Change, the Secret and the community pages hold the reader up between those moments. Progress saves on the reader's own device.

Why it exists
Because guilty people hide

A hidden sin deepens — you stop talking about it, and then you stop talking to Allah about it. The site gives the reader a private way to understand the why (love first, not fear), to act at the moment of temptation, and to prove the one thing they doubt most: that they can change.

Two beliefs run under every tool: obedience follows love, not fear — so the site teaches Allah's mercy before any ruling. And the nafs can be retrained — it is not a fixed fate but a muscle.

Section 01 · Week One

Week 1 — “A Question Worth Asking”

Seven short readings, shown one paragraph at a time. Week 1 is built in three parts, in this exact order.

Part 1 — Set the background: make the reader realise how much Allah SWT loves us

Method 1
Pointing out His signs

Your first breath before you earned anything. Your heart beating without your permission. The sun rising with precision, the rain, the seed splitting and becoming a tree. Every leaf and star is a sign — you are a deliberate creation, not an accident.

Method 2
Remembering the moments Allah saved them

When everyone left — who was there? Allah. Who kept your heart beating at rock bottom? Allah. Verse after verse: “O My servants who have transgressed against themselves…” (39:53), and the hadith that Allah is more merciful to His servant than a mother to her child.

Method 3
Inculcating love of Allah

“We are usually taught to fear Allah — and barely ever to love Him.” So before any command, the heart is turned to love, by the will of Allah, because obedience flows from love — not fear alone.

The purpose: map the “trigger → feeling → payoff → gap” pattern of their own brain — and prove they can be consistent. (“Everyone says Gen Z / Gen Alpha can't do anything consistently. Let's prove them wrong.”)

#The questionPurpose behind it
1What part of the day do I listen to music the most?Finds the time-slot — morning, commute, study, before bed
2How do I feel or think before listening?Names the state — bored, anxious, sad, excited, or plain habit
3How do I feel after listening?Tests whether music actually helps — better, same, worse, numb?
4What kind of music was it?Captures genre, artist, song — the detail makes it real
5How would my day have been different if I had not listened?Questions the imagined payoff — would anything actually change?

Section 04 · The Inner Defence

The Excuses Tool — “Catch the voice inside your head”

Four classic excuses that stop change. Each one opens into four tabs — The Thought · The Causes · Why It's Wrong · The Solution — so a relapse never goes unexplained. A progress bar (“You have explored 0 of 4 excuses”) tracks how many the reader has honestly taken apart.

Excuse 01

“I got hooked on it when I was younger — I can't help it.”

Cause: self-deception; placing the blame outside yourself (external locus of control). Why wrong: 4:79 — whatever hits you is from yourself; 91:7–8 — Allah gave you a conscience. Solution: neuroplasticity — the brain rewires with practice (like the habit of brushing your teeth being built once, day by day).

Excuse 02

“Allah will never forgive me.”

Cause: not knowing the breadth of Allah's mercy; a cognitive distortion — all-or-nothing guilt. Why wrong: 39:53 “despair not of Allah's mercy”; 11:114 — good deeds wipe away bad; 9:40 — Allah is with the believer even in the cave. Solution: the 4-4-4 trick — breathe 4 s in, out; write 4 things you're thankful for; commit 4 weeks of a small healthy practice. Ibn Qayyim's bird: forgive yourself the way the fledgeling shakes the dust off.

Excuse 03

“Just one last time.”

Cause: the dopamine tug-of-war — your limbic system wants the quick reward while your prefrontal cortex (the planner) knows better. Why wrong: death isn't on your schedule — 2:281 “then to Allah you shall return”; 59:18 — let each soul look at what it has sent ahead. Solution: the hot-stove method — vividly feel the burn of the last time, make the consequence real before acting.

Excuse 04

“Allah will forgive me anyway.”

Cause: seeing only one face of Allah — mercy without the Just (Al-Qahhar) — presumption. Why wrong: the balance is hope AND fear — 3:133, 78:40. Solution: neither despair nor presumption — sincere istighfar that is followed by a visible action.

Why the four-tab layout matters: the reader can't skip. To see the solution they must first read the thought, then its causes, then why that thinking is wrong — so the excuse is dismantled from its root instead of just argued against at the surface.

Section 06 · Measuring the Journey

How progress is tracked

Three layers — a public feedback channel, built-in in-app trackers, and a planned anonymous questionnaire that will map the whole journey over time.

Layer 1 · Live today

Google Form, linked from the site

The Feedbacks & Testimonials page embeds a Google Form (docs.google.com/forms/…viewform) so the reader can rate and comment anonymously. Per the privacy note it collects only the tool, a rating and a message — no identity, no data trail.

Layer 2 · Live today

Trackers inside the tools

The Music tool keeps a day-by-day 7-day streak with a flame counter and stores each night's journal answers — all saved quietly in the reader's own browser (localStorage).

Layer 3 · Planned

The tracking questionnaire

Next step: a short optional, anonymous check-in questionnaire — on the same Google Form page — asking for numbers the site already generates: days clean, urges survived, protocol steps completed, and how fast a slip was followed by repentance. Over a few check-ins this turns feel-good into a measurable trend the mentor can read.

What the questionnaire will collect (draft, from what the tools already measure): days clean this month · urges that actually surfaced · how many were ridden out with the 16 steps · days since the last slip · and — after a slip — time until tawbah and return. Enough, told honestly, to watch the arc.
